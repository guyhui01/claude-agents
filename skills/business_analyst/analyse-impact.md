# Skill — Impact Analysis & Change Management

> Certifications: **PROSCI Change Management Practitioner** · **ACMP CCMP** (Certified Change Management Professional) · **IIBA CBAP/AAC** · **PMI-PBA**
> Agent: AGENT-BUSINESS-ANALYST.md

## Objective

**Systematically** measure a project's impacts on the organization (5 POOCC dimensions), map stakeholders (Mendelow Power × Interest), analyze driving forces vs resistances (Lewin Force Field), structure change steering (Kotter 8 Steps · Lewin · Bridges · PROSCI ADKAR · McKinsey 7S) and secure governance via the CCB — in order to guarantee measured **sustainable adoption** rather than merely the technical go-live.

## Kotter 8 Steps for Leading Change (J. Kotter, HBR 1995 / book 1996)

| # | Step | Key goal | PO/BA deliverable |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Create a sense of urgency** | Establish the evidence for "why change now" | Burning platform statement, fact-and-figures dataroom |
| 2 | **Build a guiding coalition** | Form a core of legitimate leaders | Sponsor map, coalition RACI, charter |
| 3 | **Form a strategic vision & initiatives** | Clear, simple, memorable vision + initiatives | Vision Statement (1 page max), 3-5 priority initiatives |
| 4 | **Enlist a volunteer army** | Engage broadly beyond the coalition | Cascaded comms plan, ambassadors, rollout kit |
| 5 | **Enable action by removing barriers** | Remove structural obstacles (process, tools, hierarchy) | Obstacle list + removal plan, explicit mandates |
| 6 | **Generate short-term wins** | Demonstrate traction in 6-18 months | Measurable quick-wins roadmap, public celebrations |
| 7 | **Sustain acceleration** | Capitalize, go further, don't let up | Rollout waves, HR/finance integration |
| 8 | **Institute change** | Anchor change in culture and routines | HR policies (training, appraisal, bonus), rituals |

**Kotter "Accelerate" (2014, dual-OS)**: for fast transformations, run the **classic hierarchy** (run, execution) alongside a **cross-cutting agile network** (volunteer army) — useful in multi-year digital transformation programs.

## Lewin 3-Phase Model (Kurt Lewin, 1947) — founder of change management

| Phase | Mechanism | Operational lever |
|---|---|---|
| **Unfreeze** | Unlearn the current state, create the need to change | "Burning platform" communication, hard data, exposure to the risks of inaction |
| **Change** | Learn and experiment with the new ways of working | Training, coaching, sandbox, support, right to make mistakes |
| **Refreeze** | Consolidate the new state, make it the new norm | Durable adoption KPIs, managerial rituals, anchoring in HR policies |

## Bridges Transition Model (William Bridges, *Managing Transitions* 1991)

The **psychological** side of change (vs Lewin's organizational focus) — distinguishes the **change** (external event) from the **transition** (internal process experienced by people).

| Phase | Experienced state | Managerial response |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Endings** | Grieving the old, loss of professional identity | Acknowledge the loss, celebrate the past, make explicit what disappears |
| **2. Neutral Zone** | Confusion, uncertainty, temporary productivity loss | Sustained communication, ritualization, right to make mistakes, psychological support |
| **3. New Beginnings** | Engagement, rebuilt identity, restored productivity | Celebrate small wins, clarify new roles, anchor learnings |

**Bridges rule**: no transition succeeds if the Endings phase is skipped — overlooking the grieving of the existing state is one of the main causes of failure in change projects (cf. PROSCI Best Practices in Change Management 12th ed., 2023).

## PROSCI 3-Phase Process + ADKAR (Jeff Hiatt 2003)

### 3 methodology phases

1. **Phase 1 — Prepare Approach**: define success, impact, change-management approach (ADKAR baseline, Risk Assessment)
2. **Phase 2 — Manage Change**: execute the plans (Sponsor, Communication, Coaching, Training, Resistance Management)
3. **Phase 3 — Sustain Outcomes**: sustain the change (Adoption Tracking, Reinforcement, Transition to Operations)

### ADKAR — individual model (each person goes through the 5 milestones)

| Milestone | Question | Weak diagnosis | Action plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** Awareness | Do they know why the change? | Missing info, unclear comms | Targeted communication + hard facts |
| **D** Desire | Do they want to take part? | Resistance, fear, personal interest misaligned | Visible sponsorship, negotiation, transition package |
| **K** Knowledge | Do they know how to change? | No training, insufficient documentation | Training plan + supports + tutorials |
| **A** Ability | Can they actually change? | No practice, unfavorable environment | Coaching, sandbox, mentoring, right to make mistakes |
| **R** Reinforcement | Are they supported over time? | No KPI, no recognition | Adoption tracking, celebrations, bonus KPIs, rituals |

**ADKAR scoring**: 1-5 scale per milestone, measured individually or by population. Any score < 3 = **barrier point** triggering a targeted action plan.

## McKinsey 7S Framework (Waterman, Peters, Phillips, HBR 1980)

Cross-cutting **organizational** impact analysis — every transformation must align 7 interdependent levers:

### Hard Elements (tangible, manageable by leadership)
- **Strategy** — Vision, action plan, resource allocation
- **Structure** — Org chart, reporting lines, division of labor
- **Systems** — Processes, procedures, IT tools, KPIs, reporting

### Soft Elements (cultural, slower to transform)
- **Shared Values** — Culture, mission, purpose, core values (at the center)
- **Skills** — Key competencies, distinctive collective know-how
- **Style** — Management style, leadership, decision-making mode
- **Staff** — HR policy, recruitment, development, motivation

**7S rule**: a change of Strategy without aligning the Soft Elements (Skills/Style/Staff/Shared Values) **systematically fails**. Mapping the AS-IS / TO-BE gap across the 7 dimensions = an anti-superficial checklist.

## Stakeholder Impact Matrix — Mendelow Power-Interest Grid (1991)

Classifies stakeholders along 2 axes: **Power** (ability to influence the project) × **Interest** (interest in the subject):

```
                  HIGH POWER
                       |
       Keep Satisfied  |  Manage Closely
                       |
   LOW ─────────────────────────── HIGH INTEREST
                       |
       Monitor         |  Keep Informed
                       |
                  LOW POWER
```

| Quadrant | Engagement strategy | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Manage Closely** (High Power, High Interest) | Co-construction, frequent committees, validation at each milestone | Executive sponsor, HR Director, national unions, sector regulator |
| **Keep Satisfied** (High Power, Low Interest) | Targeted information, don't overload, alert on risks | Board of directors, audit committee, COMEX excl. sponsor |
| **Keep Informed** (Low Power, High Interest) | Regular transparent communication, listening, feedback loops | End users, frontline managers, consumer associations |
| **Monitor** (Low Power, Low Interest) | Minimal watch, standard communication | Out-of-scope providers, secondary IT vendors |

**Anti-pattern**: a sponsor classified Manage Closely who disengages → reclassifying as Keep Satisfied fixes nothing. Projects with **excellent change management** are **8× more likely** to meet their objectives than those with poor change management (PROSCI Best Practices in Change Management, 12th ed., 2023 — study of 10,800+ professionals in 101 countries over 25 years). Active, visible sponsorship is one of the **top contributors to success** measured.

## Force Field Analysis (Kurt Lewin, 1943) — quantifying resistances

A visual method to map the forces favorable (Driving) and unfavorable (Restraining) to a change, with a 1-5 rating per force:

```
   Driving Forces           Restraining Forces
   →    Force A (4) ──►  ◄── Force X (5)
   →    Force B (3) ──►  ◄── Force Y (3)
   →    Force C (5) ──►  ◄── Force Z (4)
   Total: 12               Total: 12
```

**Action**: if total Driving ≤ total Restraining → the project is at risk. Selectively remove the top-3 Restraining (often more effective than adding Driving forces) before kickoff.

## 5-dimension impact analysis — **POOCC** matrix

| Dimension | Question | Rating 1-5 | Quantitative indicator |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| **Process** | How many business processes change? At what level? | 1-5 | # processes redesigned + % of job time impacted |
| **Organization** | Which roles, teams, org charts evolve? | 1-5 | # employees with a modified role + # positions created/removed |
| **Tools** | Which IS tools are replaced / added / modified? | 1-5 | # applications impacted (cf. IS mapping, 3 circles) |
| **Skills** | What training is needed? At what scale? | 1-5 | Training hours/person + % of staff to certify |
| **Culture** | Which behaviors must change? | 1-5 | Gap between target and current values / postures |

**Rule**: any score ≥ 4 on ≥ 2 dimensions = a project requiring a **dedicated** change-management plan (vs a simple communication plan).

## CCB — Change Control Board (project governance, PMBOK 7 / PRINCE2)

A formal body for arbitrating project **change requests**:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **Composition** | Sponsor (Accountable) · BA (Consulted) · IS Architect · Business representatives · PMO · IT |
| **Frequency** | Weekly or biweekly depending on project intensity |
| **Inputs** | Change Request (CR) with: description, justification, impact (cost/schedule/quality/risk), BA recommendation |
| **Outputs** | Traced decision: Approved / Rejected / Deferred / Need More Info |
| **Arbitration criteria** | Business Case alignment · triple-constraint impact · residual risk · team absorption capacity |
| **Traceability** | Change Log register + back-link to the impacted backlog/requirements |

## Adapted Kübler-Ross emotional curve (1969) — 7 phases of change

```
Shock → Denial → Anger → Bargaining → Depression → Acceptance → Commitment
```

Identify which phase **each target population** is in (never homogeneous) and adapt the actions: Denial phase → hard data · Anger phase → union listening · Bargaining phase → scope negotiation · Acceptance phase → onboarding champions.

## Sector-specific worked example — Major European energy company

**Anonymized context**: major European energy company (~30,000 employees, 4 European countries, ~€70B revenue, 35M retail customers + 1.5M B2B). **"Customer Experience 2025"** digital transformation program: unified B2C CRM overhaul (replacing 4 legacy CRMs inherited from mergers) + self-care portal + mobile app + conversational AI chatbot + unified advisor workflow (350 screens → 1 interface).

**Impacted populations**:
- 8,500 customer advisors (5 call platforms + 12 French regions)
- 1,200 field sales advisors
- 320 frontline managers
- 4 representative national unions

**POOCC impact matrix analysis (1-5 rating)**:

| Dimension | Score | Justification |
|---|:---:|---|
| Process | 4 | 17 processes redesigned (KYC, contracting, complaints, mediation, collections, relocation, etc.) |
| Organization | 3 | 320 managers to upskill in digital, no hierarchical reorganization |
| Tools | 5 | Full replacement (4 legacy CRMs + 350 self-service tools → 1 unified platform) |
| Skills | 4 | 80h training/advisor (internal certifying path over 6 months) |
| Culture | 4 | Shift from oral to digital-first culture, visible real-time KPIs, omnichannel |

→ **Average score 4.0/5**: a program requiring a dedicated change-management plan (multi-lever ADKAR + Kotter 8 steps + 7S).

**Force Field Analysis**:
- Driving (total 17): competitive pressure from new entrants (5), customer NPS demand (4), advisor productivity (4), time-to-resolution gain (4)
- Restraining (total 18): 15+ years of legacy-tool anchoring (5), union resistance (5), advisors' average age 48 (4), fear of de-skilling (4)
→ **Restraining > Driving**: a resistance-removal plan is a priority **before** kickoff.

**Advisor ADKAR scoring (T0 baseline measurement, 850-advisor sample)**:

| Dimension | Average score /5 | Priority action |
|---|:---:|---|
| Awareness | 4.1 | OK — transparent program comms already deployed |
| Desire | 2.3 | **Critical**: engage 50 field champions + union negotiation + transition package |
| Knowledge | 1.8 | **Critical**: 80h certifying path (e-learning + sandbox practice) |
| Ability | 2.5 | Frontline coaching + 1:5 mentoring + dedicated sandbox |
| Reinforcement | 2.0 | Monthly adoption KPIs + recognition + performance bonus |

**Stakeholder Impact Matrix (Mendelow)**: COMEX + 4 national unions + Group HR Director → Manage Closely (10 actors, weekly committee) · Board of directors + CRE regulator → Keep Satisfied · 8,500 advisors + consumer associations → Keep Informed · out-of-scope IT providers → Monitor.

**24-month change-management budget plan**:
- Training (80h certifying path × 8,500 advisors): €6.5M
- Multi-channel communication (internal + champions + ambassadors): €1.2M
- Coaching for 50 champions + mentoring: €2.8M
- Sandbox + support measures + psychological support: €1.5M
- **TOTAL: €12M over 24 months**

**Measured gains at T+18 months after national go-live**:
- Unified CRM adoption: 65% (T+6) → **89% (T+18)** — 85% target exceeded
- Customer NPS: 28 → **46 pts** (+18 pts)
- Cost/contact: €12.8 → **€10.0** (-22%)
- Average resolution time: 11 min → **6.5 min** (-41%)
- Advisor satisfaction (internal eNPS): 6.2/10 → **7.1/10** (+14%)
- Advisor turnover: 14% → **9%** (-5 pts)
- **Program ROI: 22 months** (OPEX saved > transformation investment)

## 8 impact-analysis & change-management anti-patterns

- ❌ **Starting a project without an impact analysis** ("we'll see later") → projects with poor change management have a markedly lower objective-attainment rate (cf. PROSCI Best Practices 12th ed., 2023: 8× less likely than those with excellent change management)
- ❌ **One-dimensional impact analysis** (only process, ignoring culture & skills) → partial POOCC = blind spot
- ❌ **Missing stakeholder mapping or misidentified sponsor** → fragile coalition, impossible trade-offs
- ❌ **ADKAR skimmed without individualized scoring per population** → an ineffective one-size-fits-all plan
- ❌ **Top-down-only communication plan** (no feedback loop) → undetected silent resistance
- ❌ **Missing quick wins** (Kotter step #6 ignored) → coalition runs out of steam at 6-12 months
- ❌ **No measurable adoption indicator** (login rate ≠ real adoption) → false success signals
- ❌ **Executive sponsor absent or disengaging post-kickoff** → orphaned project; active/visible sponsorship identified as the **top contributor to success** in PROSCI Best Practices 12th ed. (2023)

## Output format

For each impact-analysis engagement, specify:
- **Project nature**: IS · process · organizational · regulatory · AI · multi-dimensional
- **Population scope**: number, jobs, geographies, average tenure
- **Target timeline**: fast transformation (< 12 months — Kotter Accelerate dual-OS) · multi-year (12-36 months — classic Kotter)
- **Estimated resistance level**: low (< 20% of population) · medium (20-50%) · high (> 50%, structured social dialogue required)
- **Frameworks leveraged**: Kotter 8 steps · Lewin 3-phase · Bridges Transition · PROSCI ADKAR · McKinsey 7S · Mendelow · BABOK KA #9/#6
