# Skill — User Testing
> Certifications: UXQB CPUX-UT · NN/g UX-C

## Objective
Assess the usability of an interface by observing real users complete tasks.

## Test types
| Type | Moderated | Unmoderated | Exploratory |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presence | Facilitator present | Automated tool | Free |
| Tools | Teams, Lookback | Maze, UserTesting | Free session |
| Participants | 5-8 | 20-30 | 3-5 |
| Lead time | 1 week | 48h | 2h |

> **"5 users" rule** (Nielsen & Landauer 1993; Nielsen 2000): 5 participants reveal most of the problems in **iterative qualitative** testing. To be nuanced: actual coverage varies widely by case, and Rolf Molich's CUE studies show low agreement between teams. Increase the sample for a heterogeneous audience or a quantitative/summative test.

## Structure of a moderated session
1. Welcome & consent (5 min)
2. Context questions (5 min)
3. Task scenarios — think-aloud (30 min)
4. Post-test questionnaire: SUS (5 min)
5. Open debrief (5 min)

## Writing task scenarios
- Realistic and contextualized (no hints about the UI)
- Measurable: success / failure / abandonment
- Example: "You have just received an order. Find it and cancel it."

## Deliverables
- Facilitator guide + task scenarios
- Observation grid (task × participant)
- Summary report: success rate, SUS, key verbatims, prioritized recommendations

## Output format
Specify: prototype or live product · number of participants · lead time · desired method

## Sources
- **Jakob Nielsen & Thomas K. Landauer** — *A Mathematical Model of the Finding of Usability Problems* (CHI 1993); Nielsen, *Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users* (NN/g, 2000)
- **Rolf Molich** — *CUE (Comparative Usability Evaluation)* studies — between-team variability
- **K. Anders Ericsson & Herbert Simon** — *Protocol Analysis* (1984) — foundation of think-aloud
- **John Brooke** — *System Usability Scale* (1986/1996) — post-test questionnaire
- **Steve Krug** — *Rocket Surgery Made Easy* (2009) — lightweight usability testing

## Anti-patterns
- Asking leading questions or helping the participant during the task (facilitator bias)
- Presenting the 5-users rule as a universal statistical truth
- Testing without a realistic scenario (tasks that give away the solution)
- Confusing stated opinions with observed behavior
- No consent / GDPR for recording the sessions
