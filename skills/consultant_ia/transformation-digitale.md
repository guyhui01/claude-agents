# Skill — Digital Transformation Support through AI
> Certifications: PROSCI · CAP IABAC · PMI-ACP · AI+ Business

## Objective
Lead an organization's digital transformation by embedding AI as a strategic lever, managing change at two levels — **individual** (adoption) and **organizational** (alignment) — and ensuring lasting value creation. Transformation rarely fails on the technology: it fails on adoption, governance, and strategy/organization alignment.

## Methodological framework
- **PROSCI ADKAR** (Jeff Hiatt — *ADKAR: A Model for Change in Business, Government and Our Community*, 2006; model designed 1996) — **individual** change: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement.
- **Kotter — 8 steps** (*Leading Change*, 1996), evolving toward the **8 accelerators** and the *dual operating system* (*Accelerate*, 2014) — **organizational** change: urgency, coalition, vision, communication, removing obstacles, quick wins, consolidation, cultural anchoring. ADKAR (people) and Kotter (organization) are **complementary**.
- **McKinsey 7S** (Peters, Waterman, Phillips, Pascale, Athos — McKinsey, *Structure Is Not Organization* 1980 / *In Search of Excellence* 1982) — alignment diagnostic across 7 levers: Strategy, Structure, Systems, Skills, Style, Staff, Shared values. Check that no "S" remains misaligned.
- **Westerman Digital Mastery** (Westerman, Bonnet, McAfee — *Leading Digital*, HBR Press, 2014) — two dimensions (digital capability × leadership capability) → 4 profiles: *Beginners, Fashionistas, Conservatives, Digital Masters*.

## The 4 pillars of AI transformation

### 1. Strategy & Vision
- Define the 3-year AI ambition aligned with the business strategy.
- Identify the priority value domains (coupling with the roadmap).
- Build the overall business case.
- Secure C-level sponsorship (Kotter urgency #1).

### 2. Organization & Skills
- Create or reinforce the AI structure (CoE, product teams).
- Define the key roles: CDO, Lead Data, ML Engineers, AI PO.
- Training and upskilling plan (ADKAR's Knowledge + Ability).
- Recruitment and strategic partnerships.

### 3. Data & Technology
- Audit and clean-up of the data estate (a non-negotiable prerequisite).
- Infrastructure modernization (cloud, data platform).
- Selection and deployment of AI tools.
- MLOps and model governance.

### 4. Culture & Change Management (PROSCI ADKAR)
```
Awareness     → Why change? (communication, sponsorship)
Desire        → Wanting to change (involvement, perceived benefits)
Knowledge     → Knowing how to change (training, documentation)
Ability       → Being able to change (tools, support, practice)
Reinforcement → Anchoring the change (visible successes, recognition)
```

## AI transformation maturity model
| Phase | Name | Typical duration *(indicative)* | Markers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Awareness** | 1-3 months | Leadership convinced, budget allocated |
| 1 | **Experimentation** | 3-6 months | 2-3 PoCs launched, data team formed |
| 2 | **Industrialization** | 6-18 months | Use cases in production, ROI measured |
| 3 | **Differentiation** | 18-36 months | AI in the key processes |
| 4 | **Continuous intelligence** | 36+ months | AI at the heart of the strategy |
> **Indicative** durations: the real pace depends on data maturity and sponsorship, not on a theoretical calendar.

## Key success factors
- **Strong sponsorship**: CEO or CDO as executive sponsor (otherwise the transformation runs out of steam).
- **Visible quick wins**: demonstrate value in < 3 months (Kotter #6).
- **Data governance**: reliable data before the models.
- **Talent & culture**: invest as much in people as in technology.
- **AI governance**: AI committee, AI Act compliance, ethics.

## Example — Construction group (anonymized)
**Context**: multi-site construction group, low-digital field culture. Initial Westerman profile: *Conservative* (low digital capability, engaged leadership).
- **7S — detected misalignments**: *Skills* (data) weak, *Systems* heterogeneous per site, *Shared values* "digital is for headquarters."
- **Kotter**: urgency created by margin pressure + site safety; coalition of headquarters + works directors; quick win = assisted quantity *takeoff* estimation.
- **Field ADKAR**: Desire and Ability are the weak links → site ambassadors, simple mobile tools, hands-on local support.
- **Roadmap**: H1 assisted estimation/quotes; H2 delay prediction + site safety via vision; H3 group data platform.

> **Illustrative** elements: to be recalibrated to the context.

## Anti-patterns
| Pitfall | Symptom | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Tech-first** | AI deployed without adoption | Start from the business need |
| **PoC graveyard** | 10 PoCs, 0 in production | Go/No-Go criteria + industrialization roadmap |
| **Data quality last** | Biased or inaccurate models | Data audit before any PoC |
| **Change management forgotten** | Resistance, low adoption | ADKAR + Kotter from day 1 |
| **No governance** | Regulatory risks (AI Act) | AI committee + written AI policy |
| **7S misaligned** | AI strategy with no org/skills following | 7S diagnostic, address each lever |
| **Weak sponsorship** | A program that runs out of steam | Executive sponsor + shared urgency |

## Deliverables
- Maturity diagnostic + 7S + Westerman positioning (D1)
- 3-year AI transformation plan (D2)
- ADKAR change-management plan + Kotter sheet (D3)
- Transformation steering dashboard (D4)
- "AI for all" training / awareness program (D5)

## Output format
Specify: current transformation phase · sector · size · budget · horizon · main identified resistances · estimated Westerman profile · executive sponsor.
